How it was tested

  1. Freeze real examplesItems drawn from your own database: 482 headline pairs, 499 articles with themes, 295 headlines by type, 50 searches, 150 stories, 18 filings and 55 Ask questions with their exact context.
  2. Write the right answers firstBefore any model ran: are these two headlines the same story? Which articles answer this search? Which key facts must a summary include? What must an Ask answer say, and what must it not?
  3. Run every candidate on the same itemsEach model or prompt saw identical inputs, so differences come from the model, not the data.
  4. Grade blindAnswers were shown under shuffled letters, so the grader didn't know which model wrote which. Numbers not found in the source were flagged automatically.
  5. Choose on one part, confirm on the otherAbout a third of the items were held back. Choices were made on the rest, then checked on the held-back items, so a win isn't just luck on the examples used for tuning.

      What was wrong, and what fixed it

      The mistakes were mostly about reading the context, not missing knowledge. So the fix was to make the context say things plainly, and to add a short rules paragraph to the prompt.

      Sign flips
      "+28%" was sometimes reported as a loss. The context now says "+28% above cost" and "ahead of the S&P 500 by 3.2 points".
      Weekend dates
      Moves were placed on Saturdays because articles are dated when published. Every date now shows its weekday, and price moves name their trading day.
      Misread dollars
      "$1,000" next to a recent buy was read as profit. It now says "($1,000 invested)".
      Ranks and labels
      Exposures are numbered #1, #2…; section names written like citations ("[HOLDING DETAIL]") are removed from answers.
      Prompt rules added
      "If it doesn't answer part of the question, say so… Read the figures exactly as given… Keep each figure tied to what it measures… never place a market move on a weekend."

      Qwen3.6-35B made far more factual errors (7 vs 1 on the new questions, even with the tuned prompt) and answered more slowly, so Ask stays on Qwen3.8-27B. Answers take ~17–25 s; the changes didn't slow them down.

          Run timeSummaries take ~3.5 minutes longer per run; runs now take about 9–13 minutes.

          One graderGrading was done by one reviewer (Claude), blind to the model; partial facts were counted generously and the same way for every model.
          Small held-back sets15 Ask questions and 44 stories: big differences are reliable, small ones (a few points) are not.
          One run eachEach model answered each item once; a second run could differ a little.
          FallbackGemma models stay configured as backups if Qwen isn't available; the old database is kept until mid-November in case the embedding change needs undoing.

          Threats to validity

          1. One annotator, one grader, same model family. Claude wrote every label and graded every free-text answer, with no second human or model and no inter-annotator agreement. Labels were written from the source text before any candidate output was seen. Grading was blind to the model. Self-agreement on a blind re-grade was {{ t.stories.regrade.same }}/{{ t.stories.regrade.n }}, which bounds noise, not bias.
          2. Tuner = grader for Ask. The same agent wrote the checklist, studied the tuning failures and graded the result. The 15 questions written afterwards limit the risk of teaching to the test, but they're few.
          3. Small held-out sets. Ask {{ t.ask.sets.holdout }}, stories {{ t.stories.sets.test }}, filings {{ t.filings.sets.all }}. Effects under ~5 pts of coverage, or a few wrong facts, are not resolvable.
          4. Selection on all items for the embedder. Model choice used threshold-free metrics on every item, so its "held-out" numbers aren't fully clean. The effect against EmbeddingGemma is large, and the top three candidates are tied anyway.
          5. One sample per item. Temperature 0, but llama.cpp batching isn't bit-for-bit deterministic, and run-to-run variance wasn't measured.
          6. No multiple-comparison correction across the ~10 decisions on this page. Read the borderline ones (search nDCG) accordingly.
          7. Distribution. Items come from two weeks of news (Oct 18 – Nov 1, 2026) for this one portfolio, in the middle of Q3 earnings season, so earnings coverage is over-represented.
          8. Recall-type metrics. Coverage counts reference facts present; precision is only the count of unsupported statements and invented numbers. Readability and redundancy weren't scored.
          9. Shared hardware. Latency depends on what else the GPU is serving; the numbers are medians from one day each.
