How Portfolio Watch works

Three times a day the server reads the news about your {{ n_stocks }} stocks and {{ n_funds }} funds, keeps what matters to you, writes short summaries, and builds the Digest. Everything runs on the local server and its shared GPU: no cloud AI sees your portfolio.

When it runs

Every day at 6 am, 2 pm and 6 pm Pacific. Next: {{ next_run }}. The times follow the US market, which is open 6:30 am – 1 pm Pacific.

6 amOvernight news and pre-market earnings, ready before the 6:30 open. Also fetches prices, analyst moves, {% if adv %}{{ adv.label }} picks {% endif %}and new ideas.
2 pmAn hour after the close: the day's moves and after-hours earnings. Fetches prices again, so you see today's close.
6 pmEarnings-call coverage, analyst write-ups and the evening wrap-ups.

The 10 steps of a run

Tap a step for details.

  1. 1
    CollectRead ~200 news sources for your holdings and themes

    RSS feeds of news sites, Google News searches per holding, Finnhub company news, SEC EDGAR filings (8-K, 6-K, Form 4), FRED macro data, Reddit, Motley Fool news{% if adv %} and {{ adv.short }} write-ups{% endif %}. Sites are asked politely: robots.txt is respected for pages the app crawls, and each site gets a few seconds between requests.

    Status → Sources in the last run
  2. 2
    Filter and tagDrop old, seen and junk items; find which of your stocks each one names

    Items older than 3 days or already stored are dropped. Company names, aliases and cashtags are matched to tickers: "Micron" or "$MU" → MU. Short tickers that are also words (MU, META) count only as $MU, (MU) or NASDAQ:MU.

  3. 3
    UnderstandTurn each article into a "meaning vector"

    An embedding model reads each headline and summary and turns it into a list of {{ emb.dim }} numbers that captures what it's about. Articles about the same thing get similar vectors, even with different words. Model: {{ emb.served_model }}AI

    Why this model
  4. 4
    Sort by typeNews, analysis/opinion, press release or call transcript

    Clear cases go by title rules ("earnings call transcript", "announces", a headline ending in "?") and by source (a press-release wire like GlobeNewswire). The rest are compared with a few example headlines of each type. The Digest uses this to keep opinion pieces in their own section.

  5. 5
    Score relevanceIs it about one of your holdings or themes?

    Each article is compared with "news about Microsoft (MSFT)" for every holding, and with each theme (Fed decision, CPI, AI data center spending…). A holding counts when the headline names it, or when the summary names it and it's the article's best match. Themes need a clearly stronger match than the other themes.

  6. 6
    Group into storiesCopies of the same news become one story

    Headlines published within 36 hours that mean the same thing are grouped ("Meta plans $20B bond sale" and "Meta seeks up to $25 billion in debt sale"). The best source becomes the story's main link; the count of sources shows how widely it was covered. Comparison headlines ("X vs. Y"), headlines naming different holdings, and analyst moves by different firms ("Baird raises Apple target" vs "UBS raises Apple target") are never merged, and two headlines about the same company must also say something similar once the company's name is taken out ("CEO maps out acquisition push" is not "an incredible growth stock"). If a different article becomes a story's main one, the story is summarized again.

  7. 7
    Fetch full textRead the whole article for the stories that will be summarized

    Only where the site allows it and isn't paywalled; otherwise the summary uses the snippet. About half succeed.

  8. 8
    SummarizeUp to {{ llm.max_stories }} stories in Digest order

    For each story the AI writes up to 3 sentences on what happened, the sentiment for your holding (positive, negative, mixed, neutral), up to 4 key numbers (each checked against the article text), why it matters, and whether the story is really about your holding or only mentions it. Model: {{ llm.model }}AI (backup: {{ llm.fallback_model }}).

    How it was chosen
  9. 9
    ExtrasFilings, prices, funds, {% if adv %}{{ adv.label }}, {% endif %}ideas

    SEC filings: press releases in 8-K/6-K are summarized; Form 4 insider trades are parsed. Market data (6 am and 2 pm): price, valuation, analyst ratings and targets from Yahoo Finance. Fund holdings: each fund's latest SEC report, so you see your real exposure through funds. {% if adv %}{{ adv.label }} (6 am): {{ adv.short }} picks and ratings. {% endif %}Ideas (6 am): {% if adv %}new {{ adv.label }} picks and {% endif %}notable insider buying in stocks you don't own.

    HoldingsIdeas{% if adv %}{{ adv.label }}{% endif %}
  10. 10
    Write the Digest, tidy upThe Digest, then old data is deleted

    The Digest is ordered by what matters to you: a heads-up (earnings, big moves, anything that needs you), company announcements, insider trades, analyst moves, then your holdings largest first, themes and funds, macro, analysis & opinion, Reddit. Afterwards articles older than {{ keep }} days and digests older than 3 days are deleted.

    Storage

    DatabaseSQLite in WAL mode (the web app reads while a run writes). Articles, stories, runs, quotes, prices, fund holdings, meta.
    Vectorssqlite-vec vec0 table, {{ emb.dim }} × float32 per article, cosine distance, for Search and Ask; headline vectors as a BLOB column for grouping. The stored model name and dimension are checked on start; a different model needs a re-embed.
    RetentionArticles and stories {{ keep }} days, digests 3 days.

    Ask, on demand

    Question → query embedding (with a retrieval instruction) → sqlite-vec KNN: up to 4 articles for each of the first two holdings the question names, then the 8 nearest overall, ≤ 1,500 chars each. Plus a structured context: positions and returns (signed, "above cost", "ahead of the S&P 500"), exposures numbered #1, #2…, fund overlap, market data with weekdays. → {{ llm.ask_model }} with the tuned prompt; answers cite sources as [n].

    Failure handling

    • Embedder not served within {{ emb.wait_seconds }} s: the run skips embedding, keeps the articles and the next run resumes them; the Digest says so.
    • Summary model not served: the fallback model is used.
    • Per-source request, error and 304 counts are kept per run (Status page). A price refresh that mostly fails is retried next run.
    • Only one run at a time (flock); the web app's own lock keeps a single server.
